CrossBo Agent Travel Hotel Workspace MVP
Product Requirements Document
A complete product baseline for hotel review, approval, operational readiness, commercial tracking and OPERA Cloud confirmation of agent-originated reservations.



This document defines the hotel workspace. It does not define the complete agent portal or replace the property management system. Product, design, engineering, operations and hotel launch teams should use the business rules and acceptance criteria as the implementation baseline.

[Heading 1] 1 Why This Product Exists
Travel agents manage guest bookings, changes, documents and communication across email, chat, spreadsheets and PDFs. Hotel teams then reconstruct that information before they can approve a stay, confirm it in the PMS, prepare for arrival or reconcile the commercial obligation. The fragmentation creates duplicated entry, unclear ownership and avoidable delays.
The Hotel Workspace gives the property one operational view of agent-originated business. It receives a structured booking request, preserves the agent and guest context, supports hotel review, and creates the approved reservation in OPERA Cloud. It then continues to show the latest operational state returned by OPERA while CrossBo owns collaboration, approval evidence, document workflow, communication and settlement tracking.
A hotel approval is successful only when OPERA confirms that the reservation was created. CrossBo must never present a local approval as a confirmed stay.
The MVP is designed around existing CrossBo and hotel data models. It introduces workflow and intelligence over those models rather than creating page-specific copies. Mock OPERA responses will initially sit behind the same backend provider contract that the live OHIP integration will later implement.
[Heading 1] 2 Expected Hotel Experience
The workspace should feel like a reliable operating desk for agent business. A user should understand what requires attention, why it matters, who owns it and what happens after an action. The product may summarize and recommend; it must preserve human authority for approvals and financial or permission changes.
Each role receives a stable Home layout whose content and priority reflect that role and the current property context.
Every Home item opens the owning module with the relevant booking, agent, transaction or document context.
A reservation manager can review an agent request, compare the submitted terms with live OPERA availability and rate data, and approve without duplicate entry.
Approval revalidates inventory and rate. No inventory is held while a request is pending.
A booking becomes Confirmed only after OPERA returns a successful reservation confirmation identifier.
Changes from the agent are explained as a comparison and returned for human approval when they affect commercial or operational terms.
Front desk users see arrival readiness and guest context without being exposed to finance information they cannot access.
Revenue and general managers can track amounts to collect or pay without CrossBo moving money in the MVP.
Chat and email activity among hotel, agent and guest is retained with its channel, participants, related booking and delivery state.
Failures preserve the request and show a safe retry, correction or escalation path.

[Heading 2] 3.2 Entry points and persistent context
Authenticated hotel users enter through Home or a direct deep link to an authorized entity. The persistent header shows the current property, hotel-local business date and time, last successful refresh, user name and profile control. The name control opens the role-aware AI briefing.
Every module preserves the active property. Where relevant it also preserves the selected date range, booking, guest, agent or agency. Today means the configured hotel business date and timezone, not the user device date.
[Heading 2] 3.3 Shared layout principle
Primary workspace pages use a four-column by two-row modular grid. Widgets may combine cells, but the grid is a design constraint rather than a data model. Detail views may use a modal or side panel when users need to retain list context. Nested overlays should be avoided; a stay opened from a guest or agent view should replace the detail layer or use a single subordinate panel with a clear back path.

[Heading 1] 6 Normal End to End Journey
1. An agent or authorized hotel user creates a structured booking request. The request identifies the agency or may exist without an agent when the hotel creates it directly.
2. CrossBo validates required request fields and records the submitted version. The request enters Approval Needed; it does not hold inventory.
3. The correct reservation users receive a Needs You signal. Home links into the request with the review context preserved.
4. The reviewer sees guest, stay, rate, source, notes, documents, conversation, prior versions and any AI-generated change summary.
5. The reviewer may reject, request changes through the agent conversation, or approve. Request Changes records structured requested changes and opens the relevant chat.
6. An agent resubmission creates a new immutable version. CrossBo compares it with the previously reviewed version and summarizes material changes for human approval.
7. When Approve is selected, CrossBo revalidates live inventory, eligible rate, restrictions and required identity or profile inputs with OPERA.
8. If validation fails, CrossBo keeps the request unresolved and offers a rate or date correction, change request or retry. It does not create a local confirmation.
9. If validation succeeds, the backend performs idempotent guest-profile matching and OPERA reservation creation.
10. Only after OPERA returns a confirmation identifier does CrossBo mark the booking Confirmed, close the approval signal and create the associated finance tracking record.
11. OPERA business events update the CrossBo projection for modifications, cancellation, check-in, check-out and no-show. Reconciliation handles missed or delayed events.
12. Document, communication, pre-check and finance journeys continue against the same booking and reservation identity through completion and history.
[Heading 1] 7 Module Widget and Workflow Journeys
[Heading 2] 7.1 Home
Home interprets information owned by other modules. It does not create a second copy of a booking, payment or message. The default widget set is stable by persona; priority and contents change as the operating context changes.

The default operational booking range is the hotel business date through the next 30 days. Users can change the range. Open enquiries without stay dates use created or last-updated date. Completed stays leave the active pipeline after the next business date. Rejected, cancelled and no-show records leave active views when terminal. Settled finance records leave active finance views. Every record remains searchable and auditable through history and date filters; removal from a widget never deletes data.
[Heading 2] 7.2 Agents
The Agents module supports individual relationship management while retaining the agency as the commercial parent. The directory lists each person separately, with the agent name primary and the agency secondary. Search covers agent, agency, email and identifiers. Filters may include status, active booking count, revenue period and relationship status.
Directory rows show active bookings and revenue in the selected property reporting period and currency.
Booking Overview shows total active agents, average booking value and average length of stay from booking data rather than stored profile fields.
Active Bookings via Agents shows the selected period and opens filtered bookings.
Communications offers Agent and Guest tabs; selecting a conversation keeps the user in the workspace.
The agent detail view provides Overview, Bookings, Rate Plans and Documents, plus a Chat action.
Commercial relationship fields normally inherit from agency: collection model, partnership status, payment terms, settlement currency, agreement, credit limit and settlement method.
Rate Plans shows only plans the selected agent or agency is allowed to use, including code, price basis, currency, static or dynamic behavior, public or private availability, and net or commissionable treatment.
[Heading 2] 7.3 Bookings
The Bookings module is the complete request and reservation history. The searchable list includes booking date, stay dates, guest, agent or direct source, booking ID, OPERA confirmation identifier when available and lifecycle state. Today Arrivals and Departures use the hotel business date. Needs You presents booking-related action signals.
Opening a booking shows the guest profile, agent or agency, notes, stay information, prior agent bookings when matched, event timeline, documents, communication and version history. The stay action set is permission- and state-dependent: Approve, Reject, Request Changes, Modify, Cancel where supported, Retry OPERA, or open the authoritative reservation context.
[Heading 2] 7.4 Finance
Finance is a settlement and reconciliation workspace. It receives an obligation automatically when a reservation is confirmed or when a confirmed reservation change alters the obligation. It does not execute a collection or payout.
The primary list is Payments or Obligations, not a duplicate bookings list.
Each row shows booking, counterparty, obligation type, gross value, commission basis, amount to collect or pay, original currency, reporting currency value, due date and status.
Authorized users can approve a settlement, mark collected, mark paid, reverse an erroneous mark with a reason, attach a reference, and open the related stay or voucher.
Import supports reconciliation inputs with validation and a preview. Export respects filters and permissions and includes original and reporting currencies.
Dashboard measures include agent booking revenue for the selected period, revenue versus commission, amount to collect and amount to pay.
[Heading 2] 7.5 Inventory
Inventory is organized by room type. Each room type exposes attributes and amenities, inventory and occupancy summary, upcoming bookings, rate codes and assignments. Physical-room information may be visible where OPERA supplies it, but the sellable product remains the room type.
Rate Lookup accepts check-in, check-out, guest counts and room type. Agent or agency context is optional: without it, results show general eligible rates; with it, the lookup also evaluates negotiated eligibility. The result remains inside the widget unless detail is needed, and it shows price, currency, restrictions, commission treatment and freshness. Rate codes may be assigned to an agency by default and, where the model permits, overridden for an individual agent. Every assignment has effective dates and audit history.
Occupancy terms must map explicitly to OPERA. Sellable inventory excludes occupied, out-of-order, out-of-service and otherwise restricted inventory according to the configured OPERA definitions. CrossBo must not infer On Hold from a pending CrossBo request because MVP requests do not hold rooms.
[Heading 2] 7.6 Settings
Settings is available to the General Manager by default and to another role only through explicit administration permission. The four by two layout contains Team at two by one, Roles at one by two, Required Documents and Voucher Templates. Settings changes are property-scoped and audited.
Team supports invite, activate, deactivate and role assignment. Deactivation must preserve audit history.
Roles supports reusable permission sets and prevents removal of the final active user with full administration permission.
Required Documents defines property-wide document types, required or optional status, allowed file constraints and whether approval is required. Conditional rules are later scope.
Voucher Templates provides three or four editable templates, preview, duplicate, archive and default selection. Exactly one active template is the property default.

[Heading 2] 7.13 Booking workflows
[Heading 3] 7.13.1 Review approve reject or request changes
1. An authorized user opens Approval Needed and confirms the version under review.
2. The page shows guest, source, stay, freshness, eligibility, price, policy, documents, notes and conversation.
3. Approve runs the no-hold OPERA sequence. Reject requires a reason and notifies the agent where present.
4. Request Changes records structured requested fields plus a message and opens the agent conversation.
5. Every outcome appears in the timeline and creates the correct downstream signal or notification.
[Heading 3] 7.13.2 Agent resubmission
1. The agent edits requested fields and resubmits.
2. CrossBo saves a new immutable version and calculates field differences.
3. AI may summarize the changes, but the reviewer can inspect exact before and after values.
4. Material changes return to Approval Needed; non-material changes follow the configured matrix.
5. Approval against an older version fails safely and reloads the latest version.
[Heading 3] 7.13.3 Hotel initiated modification
1. The user selects Modify from a booking or stay.
2. For an agent booking, CrossBo opens the conversation and a structured proposal. A direct booking uses the permitted hotel workflow.
3. The user selects fields and reviews price, policy, inventory and finance consequences.
4. Before confirmation, the change becomes a new request version. After confirmation, a material change calls OPERA only after revalidation and approval.
5. Booking, obligation, voucher and signals update only after the authoritative result.
[Heading 3] 7.13.4 Cancellation and no show
Cancellation displays current policy and financial consequence and does not update a confirmed reservation before OPERA accepts it. No-show normally arrives from OPERA. Both outcomes leave active operational pipelines, recalculate applicable obligations and preserve history.
[Heading 3] 7.13.5 Hotel direct request
An authorized user may create a request without an agent. Source becomes Hotel Direct, agent-only eligibility is omitted, and the same guest, stay, document, OPERA and finance rules apply. Adding an agent later is a material attribution change.

3. Preview separates valid, invalid and duplicate rows and shows totals.

5. Matched evidence updates obligations; unresolved rows become exceptions.

[Heading 2] 7.21 Universal minor workflows
Minor workflows are small but repeated interactions that determine whether the workspace remains trustworthy. The rules below apply wherever the same interaction appears unless a module defines a stricter rule.
[Heading 3] 7.21.1 Search
1. The user enters at least the configured minimum query length or submits the query.
2. The service searches only the active property and authorized entity fields.
3. Results identify entity type, primary label, secondary context and current state so similarly named people or bookings can be distinguished.
4. Selecting a result opens the canonical entity and records the source query only in privacy-safe analytics.
5. Clearing search restores the prior filters and sort rather than resetting the whole page.
Bookings search covers booking ID, OPERA confirmation ID, guest, agent, agency and room or room type where supported. Agents search covers person, agency, email and mapped identifier. Finance search covers obligation, booking, guest, agency and settlement reference. Inventory search covers room type, physical room where exposed and rate code.
[Heading 3] 7.21.2 Filter sort and pagination
1. The user opens filters and sees the current selection, available values and any disabled combination.
2. Applying filters updates results, counts and export scope together.
3. Sorting is deterministic and uses a stable secondary key so items do not jump unpredictably.
4. Pagination or incremental loading preserves selected filters and returns the user to the same position after closing detail.
5. Reset removes module filters but retains property and the module default date range.
[Heading 3] 7.21.3 Manual refresh and synchronization
1. The user requests refresh or the scheduled refresh begins.
2. CrossBo marks the affected widget or view as refreshing without hiding the last successful result.
3. The backend fetches or reconciles through the owning service and deduplicates concurrent requests.
4. Success replaces the projection only when the incoming version is newer and updates the freshness label.
5. Failure preserves the last known value, labels it stale or unavailable and exposes a scoped retry.
[Heading 3] 7.21.4 Open close and return
1. Opening detail retains the source module, query, filters, sort, page or scroll position and selected date range.
2. Closing a read-only detail returns directly to that source state.
3. Closing an unsaved editable view asks whether to continue editing or discard the local draft.
4. After a completed action, return preserves the view and updates the affected row or widget.
5. A deep link without source context returns to the entity default module view.
[Heading 3] 7.21.5 Notification handling
1. A source event is evaluated against notification rules and user permissions.
2. The notification records event, recipient, related entity, created time and read state.
3. Opening it resolves the destination against current permission and current entity version.
4. Mark Read affects notification state only; it does not resolve a Needs You condition.
5. If access was revoked, the destination is denied safely while the notification may be removed from the active list.
[Heading 3] 7.21.6 Audit history
1. The user opens History from an authorized entity.
2. The timeline orders human decisions, system transitions, external calls and received events consistently.
3. Each entry shows actor or system, action, business time, source and safe before and after values.
4. Technical correlation is available to support roles without exposing credentials or raw sensitive payloads.
5. Audit records cannot be edited through the product; a correction is a new event.
[Heading 3] 7.21.7 Export generation
1. The user chooses Export from the current filtered view.
2. CrossBo shows included filters, columns, date range, currency representation and estimated row count.
3. The backend rechecks permission and generates the file asynchronously when needed.
4. The user sees Preparing, Ready, Failed or Expired and can retry a failed generation.
5. The exported file has a limited availability period and the audit records requester, scope, completion and download.
[Heading 2] 7.22 Communication minor workflows
[Heading 3] 7.22.1 Start a conversation
1. The user starts from an agent, agency, guest or booking and chooses an allowed participant and channel.
2. CrossBo attaches the relevant booking or relationship context and shows which participants can see the message.
3. The user writes the message and adds only permitted attachments.
4. Sending creates a message with Queued status; the provider or in-app service returns its delivery result.
5. The thread appears in Communications and the related entity timeline.
[Heading 3] 7.22.2 Reply and switch channel
1. Reply defaults to the last valid channel but clearly identifies chat or email.
2. Switching to email requires a valid permitted address and shows the email subject and recipients.
3. Switching to chat requires the participant to have an eligible conversation identity.
4. A channel switch creates a new message in the same contextual thread while preserving the channel on each item.
5. The product never represents an email reply as an in-app chat receipt or the reverse.
[Heading 3] 7.22.3 Attachment
1. The user selects a file from the device or an authorized CrossBo document.
2. CrossBo validates file type, size, malware status and participant access.
3. A protected document is shared as an authorized reference or controlled copy according to policy.
4. Failure leaves the draft intact and identifies the rejected attachment.
5. Successful delivery records the attachment version that was sent.
[Heading 3] 7.22.4 Failed delivery retry and fallback
1. A message remains Failed or Bounced when the channel reports failure.
2. The user can correct the address, retry the same channel or select an allowed alternative.
3. Retry creates a new delivery attempt linked to the original message and does not duplicate the visible body.
4. Using a fallback channel preserves both attempts and their states.
5. A change request is not treated as communicated until at least one required route succeeds or the user records an approved offline contact.
[Heading 3] 7.22.5 Unread attention and resolution
Opening a thread may mark visible messages read for that user. Mark Unread is a personal reminder and does not create a business signal. A conversation can be marked Resolved only when no configured action condition remains; a new relevant message reopens it where the rule requires.

[Heading 1] 8 Booking Lifecycle and Approval
[Heading 2] 8.1 States and transitions

[Heading 2] 8.2 No-hold approval flow
Submission creates no inventory hold and makes no price guarantee. Immediately before approval, CrossBo requests fresh availability, rate, restriction and negotiated-rate validation. The review surface must explain any difference from the submitted request. If the exact request is unavailable, the reviewer can request changes or select an authorized alternative for the agent to accept; CrossBo does not silently substitute a room or rate.
[Heading 2] 8.3 OPERA confirmation and idempotency
Approve creates one operation key tied to the booking version. A retry reuses that key and first reconciles known external identifiers so a network timeout cannot create a duplicate reservation. CrossBo stores the OPERA confirmation identifier and correlation evidence. An unknown outcome enters Reconciliation Required and blocks another unprotected create attempt.
[Heading 2] 8.4 Modification and resubmission
Before confirmation, Request Changes records the requested fields and reason, opens the agent chat, and changes the request state. Agent edits create a version rather than overwriting the reviewed submission. AI may summarize differences as “Agent changed arrival date from 12 August to 13 August; total decreased by EUR 285,” but the visible field comparison is authoritative and a user approves.
After confirmation, contact-only edits such as a phone number may follow a lower-risk permission path when the property permits it. Material changes to dates, room type, occupancy, rate, price, cancellation terms or financial obligations require revalidation and an authorized update to OPERA. The booking remains at the last confirmed state until OPERA accepts the update.
[Heading 2] 8.5 Hotel-created and direct records
The workspace may contain a booking without an agent. Direct or hotel-created records set source accordingly and omit agent-specific rate or communication assumptions. Their existence must not prevent the same operational, document or finance views where those functions apply.
[Heading 1] 9 Intelligence and AI Behaviour
[Heading 2] 9.1 Needs You model
Needs You is an action queue, not a generic notification feed. A signal is shown only when the current user has permission and can materially resolve or route the condition.

Example rules include arrival within 24 hours with a required passport missing; uploaded document awaiting review; agent resubmitted a changed booking; approval waiting beyond the property threshold; OPERA confirmation failed; rate assignment nearing expiry; overdue collection; unread high-priority guest message; and a material operational note added by an agent.
Resolved means the underlying condition no longer exists. Dismissed records that the user intentionally removed the item without resolving it. Snooze requires a return time. Date-range changes may hide a signal from a current view but do not resolve it.
[Heading 2] 9.2 AI briefing
The user-name control presents a short briefing assembled from structured signals and approved measures. Role, permission and assigned responsibility filters run before summarization. The briefing states the business date and freshness when material, prioritizes urgent actions, and links each statement to its source context.
AI may summarize, group, compare versions, explain why a signal matters and recommend an authorized action.
AI may not create a signal without a rule or source event, change a status, approve a reservation or document, settle an obligation, assign a rate, or alter permissions.
The briefing must not expose a finance amount, guest detail or document fact that the user cannot open in the owning module.
If generation fails, the product shows the structured priority list. The workspace remains usable without AI.
Generated wording is never stored as the authoritative audit record; source facts and rule outcomes are retained.
[Heading 1] 10 Communications
The communication model supports hotel-agent, hotel-guest and agent-guest interactions. Agent-guest communication may use chat or email. The hotel can see it only where legal basis, product policy and permissions allow. Every message records sender, recipients, channel, related booking or guest, timestamps and delivery state.

Modify or Request Changes opens the relevant agent conversation with the booking attached and may prefill a structured summary that the user can edit. CrossBo must not claim an email was delivered until the provider confirms delivery. A failed email does not erase the message or mark the requested change as communicated.
[Heading 1] 11 Documents and Vouchers
[Heading 2] 11.1 Required documents
MVP requirements are property-wide. Each document type is required or optional and may require review. The booking view shows Missing, Uploaded, Under Review, Approved, Rejected, Expired or Not Required. Upload alone never means approval.
1. An agent, guest or authorized hotel user uploads the file against the booking and document type.
2. CrossBo validates file type, size, malware result and expiry metadata where applicable.
3. A required review creates a Needs You signal for an authorized reviewer.
4. The reviewer opens the file and supporting metadata, then Approves or Rejects with a reason.
5. Approval records reviewer and time and resolves the signal. Rejection notifies the appropriate party and returns the document to an actionable state.
6. A replacement preserves the prior version in audit history and enters review again.
The product should avoid storing more identity document content than necessary. Access, retention, preview and download must follow the property and platform privacy policy. Sensitive document content is excluded from AI prompts; AI may only summarize permitted metadata such as type and workflow status.
[Heading 2] 11.2 Voucher templates
Settings offers three or four starting templates. Users with template permission can edit supported fields, preview with sample data, duplicate and archive a template. One active template is the property default and is used for all users unless a user explicitly selects another active template during voucher generation. Agent-specific automatic template rules are outside MVP.
Voucher generation uses the latest confirmed reservation data and displays the template used. A voucher cannot imply confirmation when no OPERA confirmation exists. Regeneration after a material reservation change uses the current reservation version and leaves prior generated copies auditable.
[Heading 2] 11.3 Voucher purpose and authority
A voucher is a generated, versioned representation of a confirmed reservation for a named recipient and purpose. It may summarize stay, guest, agent, financial presentation and property instructions according to the selected template and the recipient permission profile. The voucher is not the reservation source of truth and cannot strengthen a requested or provisional state.
A voucher may be issued only from a reservation currently confirmed in OPERA. Every voucher remains tied to the exact reservation version and template version used to create it.
[Heading 2] 11.4 Voucher template lifecycle

Editing an active template creates a new Draft version. Publishing that version does not mutate vouchers already generated. A template cannot become active until required fields, layout validation, sample preview and supported language checks pass. The default cannot be archived or deactivated until another active template becomes default.
[Heading 2] 11.5 Voucher instance lifecycle

Downloaded is an audit event, not an exclusive lifecycle state, because a voucher can be downloaded and later sent. Abandoned preview creates no issued voucher. Superseded and Revoked documents remain available only to authorized users and display their invalid status when opened.
[Heading 2] 11.6 Voucher data contract

[Heading 2] 11.7 Generate voucher
1. The user opens Voucher from a confirmed booking or an allowed post-confirmation workflow.
2. CrossBo checks the latest OPERA reservation status, current user permission and whether required voucher fields exist.
3. The property default template is preselected; the user may choose another active template.
4. The user selects recipient type, language and permitted content options.
5. Preview renders from current confirmed reservation data and identifies the template and language.
6. The user confirms Generate. CrossBo stores an immutable voucher version with reservation and template snapshots.
7. The Generated voucher appears in booking Documents or Voucher history and the booking timeline.
[Heading 2] 11.8 Download print and copy link
1. An authorized user opens a Generated voucher and selects Download, Print or a controlled share link where supported.
2. CrossBo rechecks permission and voucher validity at access time.
3. Download or print returns the exact stored version and records an audit event.
4. A controlled link uses a revocable token, expiry and recipient scope; a plain public permanent URL is prohibited.
5. A Superseded or Revoked voucher shows a prominent invalid state and cannot be shared as current.
[Heading 2] 11.9 Send voucher by email or chat
1. The user selects Send and chooses an eligible guest, agent or agency recipient.
2. CrossBo displays channel, destination, subject or message, voucher version and any other attachments.
3. The user confirms. The voucher enters Delivery Queued and the message is linked to the booking conversation.
4. Provider acceptance changes the attempt to Sent; delivery confirmation changes it to Delivered where available.
5. Failure or bounce changes the attempt to Delivery Failed without deleting the voucher.
6. The user may retry or choose another allowed channel; every attempt remains in delivery history.
[Heading 2] 11.10 Regenerate after reservation change
1. A confirmed reservation change is received from OPERA or accepted through CrossBo.
2. CrossBo compares voucher-relevant fields with the latest Generated voucher.
3. If nothing voucher-relevant changed, the existing voucher remains current.
4. If a relevant field changed, the existing voucher is flagged Outdated Pending Regeneration and a Needs You signal may be created when it was previously sent or the stay is near.
5. An authorized user previews and generates the replacement from the new confirmed version.
6. The prior voucher becomes Superseded and remains auditable.
7. The product offers resend to previous recipients but does not silently send the replacement.
[Heading 2] 11.11 Cancellation revocation and correction
1. When OPERA confirms cancellation, every current voucher for that reservation becomes Revoked.
2. Opening or downloading the retained version visibly states that the reservation is cancelled and the voucher is invalid.
3. Where policy requires, CrossBo creates a cancellation communication task for prior recipients.
4. If a voucher was issued with incorrect template content while the reservation remains confirmed, an authorized user records a correction reason, revokes the incorrect version and generates a replacement.
5. Revocation never deletes the document or delivery evidence.
[Heading 2] 11.12 Voucher language and content variants
A template may have approved language variants. The user can select only a published language for that template. Missing translation never triggers automatic free-form translation at issue time; the user must choose another approved template or language. Recipient variants may hide internal financial fields, but they remain separate render configurations under the same template version and are testable before publication.
[Heading 2] 11.13 Voucher failure and rescue

[Heading 2] 11.14 Voucher retention access and audit
Voucher access follows property, booking, recipient and document permissions at access time.
Every preview that becomes Generated records actor, reservation version, template version, language and timestamp.
Every download, print, link creation, send, retry, delivery result, supersession and revocation is auditable.
Retention follows the approved booking and document policy. Expiry of a share link does not delete the voucher record.
Generated files and links must not reveal internal identifiers, net rates, commission or personal information beyond the approved recipient view.
Deleting a template or changing property content cannot make a historic voucher impossible to audit; the snapshot and referenced versions remain available to authorized users.
[Heading 1] 12 Finance and Currency
[Heading 2] 12.1 Tracking boundary
Finance records and reconciles obligations. It does not charge a guest, collect funds, issue a bank transfer or pay commission in the MVP.
Actions therefore use Mark Collected, Mark Paid and Approve Settlement rather than labels that imply CrossBo moves money. Each action requires the appropriate permission, amount, effective date and reference; reversals require a reason and are audited.
[Heading 2] 12.2 Calculation definitions

[Heading 2] 12.3 Currency rules
Every monetary record stores amount and original ISO currency. The property has one reporting currency for MVP dashboards. When conversion is needed, CrossBo stores the converted amount, exchange rate, rate source and rate timestamp. Aggregated values never add unlike currencies without conversion. Detail views always retain the original amount. Settlement currency defaults from the agency relationship and can be changed only with permission and audit evidence.
[Heading 2] 12.4 Lifecycle
A finance record is created after OPERA confirms a reservation. A later confirmed modification recalculates the obligation and records the delta. Cancellation and no-show behavior follow the applicable rate and commission result rather than assuming zero. Pending booking value may be forecast on dashboards but remains separate from confirmed financial obligations.
[Heading 1] 13 Roles and Permissions

Yes means included in the default role. Limited means a narrower scope that must be explicitly defined for the property. No means absent from the default role but still subject to the platform permission model. Backend enforcement is mandatory; hiding a control is not permission enforcement. Permission changes take effect promptly and cannot remove the last full administrator.

[Heading 1] 19 Acceptance Criteria

[Heading 1] 20 Decision and Launch Register
[Heading 2] 20.1 Closed product decisions
The PRD covers the hotel-side workspace for agent-originated business.
Four MVP personas are Front Desk, Reservation Manager, Revenue Manager and General Manager.
Home layouts are role-based and predictable; contextual intelligence changes content and priority.
Approval uses a no-hold model and must revalidate immediately before creating the OPERA reservation.
Approve is complete only when OPERA confirms the reservation.
Agent changes create a resubmission and an AI-assisted, human-approved comparison.
Bookings may exist without an agent.
Finance is tracking and reconciliation only.
All monetary records carry currency, and dashboard aggregation uses one property reporting currency.
Front Desk and Revenue pipelines interpret the same underlying data differently.
Needs You combines deterministic rules with AI language, not pure AI judgment.
Required documents are property-wide in MVP and have an approval workflow.
Agents can communicate with guests through chat and email.
Three or four editable voucher templates are supplied; exactly one default is used by everyone unless another is selected.
OPERA integration is backend-only, mock-backed initially, event-aware and idempotent.
Terminal items leave active views according to date rules but remain searchable and auditable.
